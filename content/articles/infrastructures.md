## 基础设施部门包括提供客运、货运、商业空间及热舒适等关键服务的社会技术系统。 <a href="#zh-b0002" id="zh-b0002"></a>

2024 年 1 月 30 日

多种科学文献强调基础设施在生命周期影响评价（LCIA）和环境产品声明（EPD）中的重要意义。资本品和基础设施是温室气体（GHG）排放总量的重要贡献来源（Frischknecht et al., 2007; Agez et al., 2022），并在富营养化潜势、土地利用、矿物和金属的非生物资源耗竭潜势以及土壤质量等其他环境影响中发挥不可忽视的作用（Brogaard et al., 2013; Brogaard and Christensen, 2016; Corradini et al., 2019）。尽管基础设施对于全面的 LCIA 至关重要，其数据也存在潜在问题：模型不确定性和过时技术可能导致 LCIA 指标的可靠性受到质疑（Tokede and Rouwette, 2023）。

### 部门概况 <a href="#zh-b0005" id="zh-b0005"></a>

ecoinvent 的基础设施部门包含 600 个数据集，覆盖三十三个地理区域，涵盖建筑、运输、农业、制造、能源生产和输送、采矿、废物处理及酒店服务部门的基础设施。该部门包含提供运输、供水与排污、能源供应以及商业或工业空间等关键服务的技术系统（[Bergesen et al., 2017](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/aa98ca/pdf)）。基础设施是消耗自然资源、制造和管理人类活动所产生产品的主要载体。因此，基础设施系统并非为直接消费而设计。不过，其作用及相关影响已纳入转化活动，以确保对生产过程进行全面核算。

ecoinvent 数据库中的基础设施部门主要通过以下一项或多项特征识别：

- 活动名称使用“construction”（建设），而非“production”（生产）；
- 清单包含土地转化和土地占用；以及
- 使用寿命结束时的退役处理纳入生产活动。换言之，使用后的产品在生产地点接受处理，例如拆除。

### <strong>部</strong><strong>门亮点</strong> <a href="#zh-b0011" id="zh-b0011"></a>

#### <strong>基础设施建设</strong> <a href="#zh-b0012" id="zh-b0012"></a>

数据库包含支持下列人类活动的基础设施系统建设数据：资源开采（采石场和矿山建设）、制造（纸和纸浆、建筑及建筑材料、化学品、电子元件）、运输、供水、废物处理、能源生产与输送、畜牧业和渔业，以及旅游住宿部门。

#### 基础设施维护 <a href="#zh-b0014" id="zh-b0014"></a>

数据库包含五个地理区域的技术基础设施系统维护数据：欧洲（RER），其中包含瑞士（CH）和德国（DE）的专门数据，拉丁美洲和加勒比地区（RLA），以及全球地理区域（GLO）。这些数据涵盖维持基础设施运行所需的资源、产品和能源的供应与运输、用水，以及废物处理。维护数据与建设数据一并呈现在同一数据集中（如鱼类腌制厂的建设和维护），或单独呈现在另一个数据集中（如道路维护）。

#### <strong>基础设施</strong><strong>寿</strong><strong>命</strong><strong>终</strong><strong>结</strong> <a href="#zh-b0016" id="zh-b0016"></a>

基础设施退役（或寿命终结）的建模纳入建设数据集。铁路轨道寿命终结的具体数据则在单独的数据集中提供。

## The sector of Infrastructure includes the socio-technical systems that provide key services like passenger or freight transportation, the provision of commercial space and thermal comfort. <a href="#en-b0002" id="en-b0002"></a>

Jan 30th, 2024

Diverse scientific sources underscore the paramount significance of infrastructure in Life Cycle Impact Assessment (LCIA) and Environmental Product Declarations (EPDs). Capital goods and infrastructures emerge as noteworthy contributors for total greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions (Frischknecht et al., 2007; Agez et al., 2022) and playing a non-negligible role in other environmental impacts, such as eutrophication potential, land use, abiotic depletion potential of minerals and metals, and soil quality (Brogaard et al., 2013; Brogaard and Christensen, 2016; Corradini et al., 2019). Despite their important role for thorough LCIA, infrastructure data also hides potential problems, as model uncertainties and outdated technology can lead to questionable LCIA indicators (Tokede and Rouwette, 2023).

### Sector Overview <a href="#en-b0005" id="en-b0005"></a>

This Infrastructure sector in ecoinvent comprises 600 datasets for thirty-three geographies, encompassing infrastructure for construction, transport, agriculture, manufacturing, energy generation and transport, mining, waste treatment, and the hospitality sector. The Infrastructure sector includes the technical systems that provide key services like transportation, water supply and sewage, energy supply and provision of commercial or industrial space ([Bergesen et al., 2017](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/aa98ca/pdf)). Infrastructures serve as the primary conduits through which natural resources are consumed and products resulting from human activities are manufactured and managed. As a result, infrastructure systems are not designed for direct consumption. However, their role and associated impacts are incorporated into transformation activities to ensure the comprehensive accounting of the production process.

The Infrastructure sector in the ecoinvent database is identified primarily by one or more of the following aspects:

- the term "construction," rather than "production," in the activity name;
- the transformation and occupation of land are included in the inventory; and
- the decommissioning at the end of the service life is addressed within the producing activity. In other words, the used product undergoes treatment at the production site, such as through demolition.

### <strong>S</strong><strong>ector Highlights</strong> <a href="#en-b0011" id="en-b0011"></a>

#### <strong>Infrastructure Construction</strong> <a href="#en-b0012" id="en-b0012"></a>

The database includes data on the construction of infrastructure systems supporting human activities in resource extraction (quarry and mine construction), manufacturing (paper and pulp, building and construction materials, chemicals, electronic components), transport, water supply, waste treatment, energy generation and transmission, animal husbandry and fisheries, as well as the tourist accommodation sector.

#### Infrastructure Maintenance <a href="#en-b0014" id="en-b0014"></a>

The database encompasses maintenance data for technical infrastructure systems in five geographical regions: Europe (RER), which includes dedicated data for Switzerland (CH) and Germany (DE), Latin America and the Caribbean (RLA), and the global geography (GLO). These data cover the provision and transport of resources, products, energy, and water use, as well as waste processing necessary for sustaining infrastructure operations. Maintenance data are presented either within the same dataset as construction data (e.g., fish curing plant construction and maintenance) or in a separate dataset (e.g., road maintenance).

#### <strong>Infrastructure </strong><strong>E</strong><strong>nd-of-</strong><strong>L</strong><strong>ife</strong> <a href="#en-b0016" id="en-b0016"></a>

The modeling of infrastructure decommissioning (or their End-of-Life) is incorporated into the construction dataset. Specific data on the end-of-life of the railway track are provided in a separate dataset.
